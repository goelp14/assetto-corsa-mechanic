# Coaching craft: what a good coach does and how to say it

How to turn analysis into improvement for *any* driver. Technique content lives in
[car-handling-theory.md](car-handling-theory.md); response rules in [pedagogy.md](pedagogy.md). Distilled from
Suellio Almeida's recorded sessions and book, and Nils Naujoks' (Popometer) sessions — the parts that make them
effective, not their specific advice.

## 1. What good coaches consistently do

1. **Listen first.** Let the driver say their background, goal, deadline and what they think is wrong. Their
   hypothesis is evidence (often half right); address it explicitly.
2. **Watch before talking.** Several laps "as if I'm not here". Take notes; look for the pattern, not the worst
   moment.
3. **Find the pattern at style level.** A driver's habits repeat in every corner of the same type (heavy braking,
   fast sweepers, chicanes). Coach the pattern, not 20 corners. On a long track this is the only way.
4. **Show, then compare.** Show a reference doing the same corner (video and traces side by side); point at the
   one difference that explains the rest.
5. **Pick the low-hanging fruit.** The biggest *mechanism*, usually upstream (entry/rotation), that will make
   later fixes possible. Defer the rest — tell the driver it's deferred, not forgotten.
6. **Explain the why (mechanism), not just the what.** "Instructing" (brake here, turn there) makes the driver
   dependent; "coaching" (why the fast driver does it, what they're trying to make the car do) makes them
   autonomous on the next car/track.
7. **Translate data into feel.** Every point needs something the driver can sense in the car: tyre noise staying
   constant, steering getting heavier, the car "pointing", where the eyes are. Telemetry that can't be felt
   can't be applied.
8. **Give 2–3 targets per corner at most** (e.g. apex, slowest point, second apex) — the corner is gone before
   five notes are remembered.
9. **Drill immediately, with short live cues.** "Turn first, then release", "patience", "too early". One cue at a
   time, repeated.
10. **Set homework and a retest.** One thing, ~50–100 laps, then a fresh lap/replay. Measure the same metric.
11. **Normalise the dip.** New technique feels wrong and is slower at first; spinning while learning the limit is
    progress ("if you can cause it, you can prevent it"). Judge balance control first, lap time later.
12. **Never tell them to copy a telemetry trace.** Conditions never match; teach the intention and the tool.
13. **Own mistakes in your own driving too** — coaches who show their errors and corrections build trust.

## 2. Diagnosis order (cheap and decisive first)

1. Equipment/controls (pedal curve, steering ratio/FFB, posture) — one wrong setting can cap a driver.
2. Big picture: where the time goes (variance/delta by section, including the straight after).
3. Braking: reference, ramp, peak, release, consistency.
4. Rotation: where the MRP is, at what speed, how it's built (snap vs build), what share of rotation happens
   before throttle, grip gaps between braking and turning.
5. Exit: throttle timing relative to rotation, unwinding before throttle, partial throttle that doesn't accelerate.
6. Line/track use and coupling with the next corner.
7. Only then setup — and only if technique can't produce the behaviour, and only if it's faster.

## 3. Scenario playbook

| Scenario | What usually helps | How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| **Beginner / inconsistent** | Consistency first: one braking marker, one turn-in marker; separate braking and turning *before* blending them (string theory later) | Few words, one action, praise repeatability over speed |
| **Plateau (experienced, stuck)** | Find the safety net they've built (rotating late, coasting, early braking); usually entry rotation; introduce a new mechanism (brake release ↔ steering, engine braking) | "Your exits are fine; the time is in the first 20 % of the corner". Expect a temporary slowdown |
| **Consistent but under the limit (hesitant)** | Practise over-rotating in low-speed corners; induce oversteer on purpose; shorten post-slide hesitation | Frame spins as data; set a "spin it on purpose" drill |
| **Over-driver (late braker, sloppy, fast in bursts)** | Brake a touch earlier; focus on rotation and lateral grip at lower speeds/exit; rotation first, speed after | "You're faster *into* the corner and slower *out* of it"; show the speed graph after the apex |
| **Afraid of oversteer** | Light hands; induce understeer via brake release as first correction; learn to spin on purpose | Explain why the fast driver isn't reacting faster — they predict it |
| **Understeer complaints** | Check entry speed, too much/too early steering, brake sweet spot, turning in too early; driving fix before setup | "Anything wrong gives understeer" — find which one |
| **New car / track** | Rotation-first approach; find MRPs in slow corners first; test whether brake release adds or removes rotation in this car | Two references per corner: braking point and MRP |
| **Limited time before a race** | One fix with the biggest, most repeatable payoff; no new techniques that need weeks | Be explicit about trade-off: confidence over tenths |
| **Endurance / real-life risk** | Margin-aware advice; avoid "spin it on purpose" drills in real cars | Say when a sim drill is sim-only |
| **Long track (Nordschleife-type)** | Style-level patterns only; no corner list | "This happens in every heavy-braking corner" |
| **Driver reports back after homework** | Same metric, same corners; if it moved but time didn't, look downstream; if it didn't move, change the cue | Celebrate the measured change, then the next single step |

## 4. Communicating in different channels

- **Live (car on track)**: 1 short cue per corner, said before the corner, never mid-corner. No numbers.
- **Between runs (debrief)**: WHAT (cited number) → WHY (mechanism) → HOW (one drill) → what it will feel like →
  success metric.
- **Written review / bundle notes**: lead with the pattern across corners, then one worked example corner with
  video/trace references, then homework.
- **Video**: freeze-frame the moment of difference; side-by-side same position (distance-synced, not time).

## 5. Language bank

- Corner structure: "brake → closing spiral → peak rotation → opening spiral".
- Rotation: "build it, don't snap it" / "1-2-3-4-5, power" / "pay as little speed as you can for the rotation".
- Brake/steer link: "turn first, then release" / "the brake only turns the car if there's steering waiting".
- Steering: "if more lock isn't turning it, stop adding lock — touch the brake and wait".
- Throttle: "throttle first, then unwind — never the other way round".
- Track: "don't hit the apex, hit the back of it" (when they bounce off it).
- Sound: "keep one constant tyre noise from braking into the corner".

## 6. What not to do

- Symptom advice ("throttle earlier", "don't coast", "brake later") without the upstream cause.
- More than two fixes per session.
- Calm-making setup changes that cost time.
- Copying a reference's trace or numbers that came from different conditions.
- Cross-driver comparisons of raw steering angle (wheel ratio settings differ).
- Claims the data can't separate — say "hypothesis" and give the test.
