# Spa-Francorchamps (GT3) — coaching notes

Track-specific knowledge to combine with the general model in
[../car-handling-theory.md](../car-handling-theory.md). Sources: Almeida's book (La Source and Bruxelles are worked
examples), a Popometer Spa GT3 coaching session (McLaren 720S, Nils Naujoks), Coach Dave / Track Titan guides.
In-car cues live in `vre-rpy-cli` track notes (`track_pack/notes/spa.json`) so they reach coaching fixes.

| Corner | What matters | Common mistakes |
|---|---|---|
| **La Source** | Exit for Eau Rouge/Kemmel (~25 s flat). Brake ~100–120 m board, uphill compression = early full pressure OK. Book: a small, quick dip at the apex that ends abruptly — *compression to snap decompression* — grip on both edges of the dip is lower than in it; take the rotation in the dip, be careful just after it. | Braking too early then re-braking; excess steering lock (>~180° on a GT3 wheel) scrubbing speed instead of brake-release rotation; very late apex that sacrifices the whole entry |
| **Eau Rouge / Raidillon** | GT3 is flat. Compression at the bottom adds grip, crest at the top removes it. | A lift costs speed all the way down Kemmel |
| **Les Combes** | Brake with the left wheels on the kerb for a wider entry; the kerb strike adds rotation if you arrive slightly faster than feels natural; a little understeer settles a mid-engine rear | Not using the kerb; too slow → kerb unsettles → hesitation |
| **Les Combes exit (left)** | Lift and upshift at the same moment into the right-hander (higher gear = less engine braking = calmer rear) | Lifting in a low gear → big front load → car darts in; hesitant throttle afterwards |
| **Malmedy** | Line for the following short straight | — |
| **Bruxelles** | Book's double-apex example. Low-downforce V-line: earlier turn-in, elliptical line, more trail, apexes further apart; high-downforce U-line: later turn-in, rounder line, apexes closer. Slow down until mid-corner, first apex later than instinct. | Bouncing off the first apex for a long time (entry *and* exit lost); 40–50 % throttle mid-corner that stops rotation without accelerating |
| **No Name** | Brake less and let the car rotate on its own | Heavy brake + big lock, then a sudden release → snap oversteer → long TC intervention → slow on the straight |
| **Pouhon** | Use the right-hand kerb while braking; later first apex → less lock, earlier/harder power | Too much lock (~85° vs ~50° needed) through the double left |
| **Fagnes / Campus** | Compound: sacrifice the right-hander to straighten the left-hander exit; line up further right; lift + upshift together on turn-in; use the full left kerb past the column | Using only the angled kerb part (pushes wide); third gear too early (no drive) |
| **Stavelot** | Commit to throttle with lock on, unwind after | Waiting for a straight wheel |
| **Blanchimont** | Flat in GT3 | — |
| **Bus Stop** | Swing wide right before the left-hander so the car is rotated by mid-corner; brake later than feels natural | Straight line then late turn → rotation left for the exit; clipping the sausage kerb |

General Spa pattern from the session: entries lost through braking/turning mis-alignment (maximum lock while still
braking hard → snap on release), and exits lost on the long straights — sacrifice corners for straights.
